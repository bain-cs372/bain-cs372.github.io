Course Designing and Constructing Models with Multi-agent Languages
Prerequisites Senior or Graduate Standing in Computer Science
Instructor Prof. Connor Bain (connor.bain@northwestern.edu)
Lecture Time Tuseday & Thursday, 3:30pm - 4:50pm
Lecture Location Schapiro Hall Rm. 101
Textbook Wilensky, U., & Rand, W. (2015). An introduction to agent-based modeling: Modeling natural, social and engineered complex systems with NetLogo. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press.

Please note that this syllabus is subject to change prior to April 2, 2026.

This course focuses on the exploration, construction and analysis of multi-agent models. Sample models from a variety of content domains are explored and analyzed. Spatial and network topologies are introduced. The prominent agent-based frameworks are covered as well as methodology for replicating, verifying and validating agent-based models. This course can help satisfy the project course and artificial intelligence area course requirement for CS and CIS majors, and satisfy the breadth requirement in artificial intelligence for Ph.D. students in CS. It also satisfies a design course requirement for Learning Sciences graduate students, counts towards the Cognitive Science specialization and as an advanced elective for the Cognitive Science major.

Course Description

This is a hands-on seminar style course. All students will design, implement, and analyze multi-agent models. Undergraduates taking this course should understand that this course is demanding and requires the drive for independent project work. Those registering for the course without programming experience should consider reaching out to the instructor on guidance.

The course is designed for both undergraduates (300 level) and graduate students (400 level). 400 level students will be expected to do additional project work. Three main types of students are anticipated:

  • Computer Science students who would like to learn about multi-agent modeling and multi-agent languages, artificial life and/or bottom-up AI
  • Natural Science, Engineering and Social Science students who have content expertise and would like to learn how to build multi-agent models of their content domain
  • Learning Science students interested in the design of multi-agent learning environments

This course will begin with an introduction to the multi-agent language NetLogo. Students will design and implement several NetLogo models and analyze their behavioral regimes. Students will also learn to build models of interaction on social networks (or other types of networks). We will cover methodology for verifying, validating, and replicating agent-based models and comparisons with systems dynamics and equation-based models. NetLogo comes with many extensions that support a variety of additional features which students can use to create specialized models. We will study complex networks, real-time data extraction, data mining, connections to physical devices, and more in the context of existing models. We’ll also explore participatory simulations, connecting real world sensors to models, and multi-level modeling in which hundreds of models can be connected.

Main Course Tools

NetLogo is a programmable modeling environment for simulating natural and social phenomena. It is particularly well suited for modeling complex systems that develop over time. Modelers can give instructions to hundreds or thousands of independent “agents” all operating concurrently. This makes it possible to explore the connection between the micro-level behavior of individuals and the macro-level patterns that emerge from the interaction of many individuals. NetLogo lets users run simulations and “play” with them, exploring their behavior under various conditions. It is also an “authoring tool” which enables users (both novices and experts) to create their own models. NetLogo is simple enough that novices can easily run, explore and build their own simulations. NetLogo has extensive documentation and tutorials for all of its features. It also comes with a Models Library, which is a large collection of pre-written simulations that can be used and modified. These simulations address many content areas in the natural and social sciences, including biology and medicine, physics and chemistry, mathematics and computer science, and economics, linguistics, sociology and social psychology. Several model-based inquiry curricula using NetLogo have been in widespread use. HubNet is an extension of NetLogo that enables multiple users to collaborate and participate in a simulation. In a HubNet activity, each participant controls an agent or set of agents in a larger model. Several HubNet activities are also included in the models library.

Course Requirements and Grading

  • Read the assigned readings
  • Gain expertise on the NetLogo language
  • Read, post, and reply to NetLogo questions on edSTEM
  • Complete assigned homework exercises/explorations, including activities such as:
    • Revising an existing NetLogo model from the Models Library
    • Participating in collaborative simulations
    • Comparing systems dynamics models with multi-agent models
    • Incorporating social network primitives, measures and utilities into multi-agent models
  • Complete in-class assignments (short quizzes)
  • Complete the final project
    • Construct at least one polished NetLogo model and do an analysis of the model
    • Submit weekly progress reports on the final project

The final grades of the course will be assigned by a weighted average of the students’ performance across the four major assessment dimensions: 1. Class Participation (15%); 2. Assignments (In-Class “Quizzes” and outside class Homework assignments) (35%); 3. Final Project (50%).

Attendance at Class Meetings

Attendance is crucial to your success in this course. Our class meetings will be highly interactive and will be focused on creating an equitable learning environment for all attendees. Everyone receives two “missed class tokens” meaning you can miss up to two classes without any impact on your class participation grade. It isn’t necessary to provide a formal excuse for these absences. After spending your missed class tokens, each additional missed class period will negatively affect your participation grade. If you miss more than 10 class periods (of the 20 scheduled), you will receive an F as this is meant to be a seminar course.

This is an upper-level seminar course and, as such, we will be learning from each other and each other’s experiences. Critiques will be a natural part of our discussions, yet those critiques need to be constructive. Feedback should not be considered the end of an idea but rather as the beginning of a new chapter of the idea.

Academic Integrity & AI Policies

Students in this course are expected to comply with the policies found in the booklet, “Academic Integrity at Northwestern University: A Basic Guide.” For the purposes of the final paper for this class, you are free to use the grammar checking tools Grammarly and LanguageTool. However, use of any other AI tools (such as ChatGPT, Claude, etc.) are not allowed because we think their use will decrease your learning. If you have a special use case that you think would be beneficial for your learning, please reach out to the instructor ASAP.

There is a different policy with respect to the weekly assignments. There, you can use LLMs such as ChatGPT to help you write your code. If you use them for this purpose, you must acknowledge having used them and write a reflection on the experience of using them. We warn you that using LLMs for this purpose can be a mixed bag, it often writes incorrect code (and sometimes even nonsensical code).

About the Final Project

The final project is to design, implement, verify, and analyze a NetLogo model. This includes interface window, commented procedures, and a full and detailed info window. The final project also includes a paper describing and analyzing the model. For proficient Java or Scala coders there is another option for the final project, to write a NetLogo extension (There may also be an opportunity to write an extension for NetLogo Web in Javascript). All final projects must be preceded by a project proposal. You will get feedback on your proposal and once approved you can start working on your final project.

400 level students must both a) produce a more polished paper, embedded in the relevant literature, at a standard that could put it on a trajectory towards publication, and one of the following: 1) Create an additional HubNet activity that complements the NetLogo model, 2) design and implement an extension to NetLogo, or 3) incorporate some other advanced NetLogo feature (e.g., LevelSpace or interfacing with hardware such as Arduino) into a complementary model. In your final project proposal you will need to outline the option you are choosing and will receive feedback from us.

Deadlines and Late Assignments
