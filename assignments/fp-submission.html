Goal

The goal of the project is to design, implement, and evaluate a substantial agent-based model of an emergent phenomenon that is of interest to you.

What’s Due

  • Completed NetLogo model/s
  • A final paper/report (details below)
  • A virtual poster (details below)
  • A 4 minute presentation (please see the separate canvas assignment for details)

The Model(s)

  1. The code should be readable (e.g., well structured, well written, and well commented) and close-to-understandable by a person who is becoming familiar with NetLogo.
  2. The Info tab should include all the main parts in the NetLogo library models. It should succinctly and accurately describe the phenomenon being modeled. A reader who does not have any background in the subject should be able to at least garner some understanding of what is going on.
  3. The interface should be intuitive and it should be possible to use the model (at least tinker with it) without having to refer to the Info tab immediately.
  4. Refer to the NetLogo Style Guide for specific guidance on formatting.

Additional Programming Requirement for 400 level students

  1. Create a HubNet activity that complements the NetLogo model
  2. Create additional related, but substantially distinct models
  3. Incorporate some other advanced NetLogo feature (such as interfacing with hardware or using the LevelSpace, GIS, Python, or Network extension) into a complementary model

Final Paper/Report

This is the final “evolution” of the document that starts as your Final Project Design Proposal which we will discuss in detail next week. The final report should include all of the same information that the proposal contains (big picture overview, description of what can be learned, motivation/rationale for the project, description of rules/model implementation), but it should be polished and also contain substantial sections describing your results and analysis of the model.

There are three major forms your analysis can take. Choose what seems most applicable:

  • Exploration of parameter space and possible regimes (i.e. BehaviorSpace and/or other tools)
  • Comparison with real world data / reference pattern (i.e. empirical analysis)
  • Report on an actual case of someone (or some group) actually using your model. This is the preferred method of analysis for HubNet models.

Formatting

You should use the IEEE Manuscript Template for conference proceedings for the template of your paper. It’s available here at the IEEE’s conference support site in both TeX and DOCX formats. While the end product will look quite different, the content should closely match that of your Project Proposal document.

    • Model parameters

Virtual Poster

Rather than a formal in-person poster session, we’re going to try something new–a virtual poster session. This will involve two components: a video walkthrough of your model + Design and Analysis Overview

Video Walkthrough

The first component should be a walkthrough (demo) of your model. You must be visible on the screen during the demo. The easiest way to do this would be to use Zoom, sharing your screen (you can use the “overlay” model to display your silhouette on top of the model), and then recording your walkthrough.

Your walkthrough should:

  1. Introduce the Interface of your model (i.e. explain what each widget does)
  2. Demo the base behavior of the model.
  3. Show at least 2 interesting behaviors you might see in the model as you manipulate the various widgets.

The video should be a few minutes in length and be enough detail to where a person familiar with NetLogo could understand how to use the model and why it is interesting after watching your demo.

Upload your recording as an MP4 or MKV file to this assignment on Canvas.

Design and Analysis Overview

This is the written component of what would normally be presented on a static poster. It will essentially be parts of your paper but rewritten for a more general audience (think other undergraduates at Northwestern – competent in programming but not necessarily in ABM or in the field you’ve chosen to model). You shouldn’t need to create anything “new” for this section and instead just be “translating” portions of your paper. You should have the following sections:

  1. Introduction + Big Picture + Driving Question
  2. Model Design
    • Key modeling choices (what are the agents; why are they that level of granularity; what can the agents do / not do?)
    • Model Pseudocode (like in our presentations all the way back in Week 2)
    • Key model limitations
  3. Analysis
    • Why did you choose this method of analysis?
    • What experiments / studies did you perform?
    • Show the outcome of some experiments: this could be in the form of a graph, table, or other summary artifact
  4. (4XX students should have a section on their 4XX component here)
  5. Where could you go from here? (Future Work)
  6. Any references
  7. An FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions)
    • Imagine you were presenting this work in a public setting. Brainstorm at least 5 questions that a Poster Session visitor might ask and provide the question and answer as a pair. You should use these question/answer pairs as a way of showing how insightful your project is – don’t focus on surface level questions.

Upload as a DOCX, PDF, or MD file to Canvas. HTML would also be fine. Please also separately upload any screenshots or visuals as a PNG/JPG as I’ll reformat them into a course website.

Evaluation Criteria

Your project will be evaluated according to these criteria:

  • Quality of paper & model design
  • Creativity/originality of the model
  • Completeness and correctness of the code
  • Quality and clarity of the code
  • Quality, depth and thoroughness of the model analysis
  • Quality and clarity of the Info Tab
  • Adherence to the NetLogo Style Guide
  • Usability, aesthetics and clarity of the user interface
  • Timeliness/thoroughness of Design Proposal, Progress Reports, and Final Report
  • Quality of the in-class presentation
  • Quality of the demo and virtual poster explanation + FAQ
