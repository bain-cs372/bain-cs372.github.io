Activity 1. Modify the Wolf Sheep Simple Model

372 students - Choose one of three options below. 472 students - choose two of the three options below.

Option A - Spatial Location and Grass

Currently the grass is randomly distributed across the space. However, it is more realistic that areas of grass are spatially co-located, i.e., areas with more grass are likely to be near each other, and areas low in grass are near each other. Change the SETUP and/or GO procedure for the grass to reflect this.

In the INFO tab of the model, describe how this change affects the model (1 paragraph).

Option B - Adding a 3rd Species

Create a third species in the model. One interesting way to do this would be to create a species that competes with the wolves for sheep. Wolves and this third species can also interact (maybe wolves can eat them, or they can eat wolves, or both). Alternatively, choose your own 3 interacting species to model. You can use the Turtle Shapes Editor to create any needed shapes.

In the INFO tab of the model, describe how this model behaves in a paragraph.

Option C - Add Evolution

Right now, the wolf and sheep are exactly the same in every generation, but real wolves and sheep evolve over time. For instance, wolves might get faster or have better eyesight (though both of these traits might also incur a higher metabolism cost). Sheep might also get faster and might learn to avoid wolves (though again this might have a cost). Add an evolutionary mechanism to the wolf sheep model.

In the INFO tab of the model, describe your addition in a paragraph.
